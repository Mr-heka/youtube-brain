# YouTube editing knowledge profile

This is a build specification, not an imported editing knowledge base. Do not manufacture expert findings to fill these sections. Import and test knowledge only when that stage is requested.

## Questions the future knowledge should answer

| Decision area | What to research and retain | Later application check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story and episode structure | Set-up, stakes, progression, payoff, preserving what actually happened | Does the whole episode tell the intended story without inventing chronology? |
| Selecting and pacing footage | Alternate takes, useful detail, repetition, pauses, chapter boundaries | Does a cut improve understanding while preserving natural speech and meaning? |
| Explanatory visuals | Real source articles, logos, demonstrations, diagrams and archival context | Does the insert explain this sentence with the correct entity, date and claim? |
| Composition and text | Reading order, caption legibility, safe regions, movement and occlusion | Inspect real footage at entry, hold and exit, clear of people and meaningful action. |
| Motion and transitions | Purposeful reveals, continuity, chapter navigation and attention | Does the motion explain a change, rather than create arbitrary stimulation? |
| Sound and finishing | Dialogue clarity, original ambience, music, motivated effects and mix | Listen in context, check sync, clipping and dialogue intelligibility. |
| Covers and continuity | Hook clarity, accurate promise, shared authored typography | Does the cover match the episode and preserve recognisable third-party branding? |

Begin with these questions, but let reviewed sources determine page boundaries. A table of topics is not evidence of coverage. Keep unsupported domains in Known gaps. Don't force every video into identical typography timing, transition frequency or effect cadence.

## Project constraints are a separate layer

The current project's video-brand and finishing instructions govern production. Preserve its approved authored type hierarchy and chapter/caption system. Genuine third-party logos, articles, UI and product images retain their own official colours and native appearance; avoid monochrome substitutions just to match the house palette. Source identity is not factual proof.

Use footage-intelligence for actual visual review. A transcript alone cannot show a free area around a moving face or hand. Preserve original masters and the landscape-only episode scope where requested. Workshop confidentiality, alternate takes and end-of-video selection follow the actual episode brief.

A useful sound may be a click, pop, swish or tone when justified by the visible or story event. These are choices to learn and audition, not a demand to add an effect at every topic. No research summary certifies a final mix.

## Staged acceptance

1. Builder structure and local contract tests: no editing-source ingestion required.
2. Selected editing knowledge acquired and synthesised with source locators and Known gaps.
3. Actual question answers checked against source material.
4. Short edits on real footage watched and listened to against the episode brief.
5. Full edit reviewed and finished.

Keep the existing overlay brain as a limited legacy research source, not the complete editing authority. Its six-source/caption limitations must survive any future migration. Do not mark steps 2–5 complete from this profile or synthetic fixtures.
